Designing efficient architectures
around the flow of data.

My research focuses on computer architecture and dataflow design. I study how to organize computation, memory, and communication together to build efficient, scalable computing systems for intelligent applications.

My work spans memory-centric reconfigurable architectures, continuous analog dataflows, and error correction compatible with in-memory operations. Together, these inform my research on analog-digital heterogeneous computing systems.

Research interests

ARCHITECTURE & COORDINATION

Memory-centric
reconfigurable computing

Hierarchical interconnects, distributed data routing, and event-driven programming for local data processing and overlapping computation with communication. Target workloads include multi-agent reinforcement learning, neuromorphic models, and graph processing.

Related work: MCHA →
DATAFLOW & INTEGRATION

Continuous analog &
heterogeneous dataflows

Connecting computation stages with homogeneous compute-and-buffer units and dense analog storage. I study how resampling and local analog data movement support continuous processing, and how these paths can interface with digital systems.

Related work: analog buffering →
RELIABILITY & PIPELINING

Reliable
in-memory dataflows

Non-binary error correction for multivalued accumulation outputs, with coding compatible with in-memory multiply-accumulate operations. Decoder and pipeline design protect results while preserving the continuity of the computing dataflow.

Research manuscript · Open-source project

MCHA: A Memory-Centric Hierarchical Architecture for Parallel-Sequential Computing

Daijing Shi, Hongxiao Zhao, Yihan Fu, Zhan Chen, Jiayi Li, Yihang Zhu, Anjunyi Fan, Yaoyu Tao, Yuchao Yang, and Bonan Yan

A hierarchical communication fabric and data-driven programming model reduce dependence on global memory in irregular parallel workloads. A cycle-accurate simulator evaluates throughput and scalability across multi-agent, neuromorphic, and graph applications.

Advanced Intelligent Systems

Neuromorphic Denoising with Fully Analog Memristive In-Memory Computing

Daijing Shi, Teng Zhang, Yuqi Li, Yaoyu Tao, Bonan Yan, and Yuchao Yang

Homogeneous memristive units perform both computation and temporal buffering to complete analog dataflows, with output resampling to limit noise accumulation. Hardware-mapped portions of models including a VAE are evaluated in a hardware–software system.

Correcting processing-in-memory multiply-accumulate arithmetic errors with LDPC

Daijing Shi, Yihan Fu, Yihang Zhu, Anjunyi Fan, Yaoyu Tao, Bonan Yan, and Yuchao Yang

A finite-field NB-LDPC scheme unifies protection for stored data and in-memory MAC results. Decoder and continuous-pipeline design are validated on a 40 nm prototype chip for error correction and energy efficiency.
